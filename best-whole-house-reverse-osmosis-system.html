What to Look For (Quick Reference)

For the full decision framework, see our complete whole house RO buyer’s guide. The quick summary:

Capacity (GPD). Match to household size, as a rough starting point: 300 GPD for 1-2 people, 500 GPD for 3-4 people, 800-1,000 GPD for 5+ people. Size the storage tank to cover your peak-day demand.

Storage and delivery. Even “tankless” units like the iSpring RCB3P, RCS5T and CRO1000 produce water far slower than a household uses it (1,000 GPD is only 0.7 GPM), so whole-house setups need a storage tank and a delivery pump. Plan floor space for the tank.

Pre-treatment. Chlorine damages RO membranes, and hardness, iron and sediment foul them. Some units include filter stages (the CRO1000 is 4-stage and the RCS5T is 5-stage, per their listings), but at whole-house flow you may also need a softener or other pre-treatment. Your water test decides this.

Wastewater ratio. The iSpring RCB3P and RCS5T list 1.5:1 (1.5 gallons of pure water per gallon to drain). Some older or basic RO designs send more water to drain than they produce. At whole-house volumes, that difference adds up fast.

Installation requirements. Whole-house RO systems need floor space for the equipment and storage tank, drain access, electrical for the pumps, plumbing access at the main water entry, and cold-protected space (no freezing). Check the feed-pressure range on the listing, too: the CRO1000 needs 80–130 psi.

FAQ

What’s the best whole house reverse osmosis system?

For most homeowners who are sure they want whole-house RO, a pre-engineered package like the US Water Systems Defender is the simplest route, since the parts are chosen to work together; confirm specs with the maker. For larger homes building their own setup, the iSpring CRO1000 is rated 1,000 GPD.

What’s the least expensive way to get whole house RO?

Usually a bare RO unit like the iSpring RCB3P that you build a system around. But it’s a unit-only purchase: you’ll need to add a storage tank, a delivery pump, and any pre- and post-treatment your water needs. Price out the full parts list and installation before comparing it with a complete package.

Are tankless whole house RO systems better than tank-based?

For whole-house use, the distinction matters less than it does under the sink. Even the highest-capacity unit here (1,000 GPD) makes only about 0.7 GPM, so every whole-house RO setup needs storage and a delivery pump. What “tankless” units like the iSpring models add is higher production and a built-in booster pump, which refills your storage faster. They need electricity, and the CRO1000 needs 80–130 psi feed pressure.

How big a system do I need for a family of 4?

Using the USGS estimate of 80–100 gallons per person per day, a 4-person household uses roughly 320–400 gallons a day. Add some margin and you land around 500 GPD plus adequate storage. The iSpring RCS5T (500 GPD) is sized for this; for the US Water Defender, confirm the rated capacity with the maker.

Do I need a softener with a whole house RO system?

Often yes, especially with hard water. Calcium and magnesium scale on the membrane surface and shorten its life. The Aquasure bundle includes a softener; if you go with a different RO system, get a water test and ask whether you need a softener upstream as pre-treatment.

What’s the difference between residential and commercial RO systems?

Mostly capacity and intended use. Typical under-sink residential RO is rated 50-100 GPD for tank systems and a few hundred GPD for tankless. The commercial units here are rated higher: the iSpring RCS5T at 500 GPD and the iSpring CRO1000 at 1,000 GPD, which is why they come up for whole-house use. Check feed-pressure requirements: the CRO1000 needs 80–130 psi.

Can I install a whole house RO system myself?

Possible but not recommended unless you’re comfortable with plumbing (main water line modifications, bypass valve installation), electrical (pump wiring), and water-treatment system design. A pre-engineered package like the US Water Defender takes the component matching off your plate. Configured Crystal Quest systems typically require professional installation. Get at least two local installer quotes. See our whole house RO cost guide for what drives installation cost.
