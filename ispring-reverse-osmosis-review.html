About iSpring Water Systems

iSpring Water Systems sells residential and light-commercial water treatment equipment. Its RO lineup spans tank-based, tankless, booster-pump and commercial systems.

What the listings document across the lineup:

  • A top-mounted faucet. The RCC7AK, RCC7AK-UV and RCC1UP-AK listings describe a top-mounted faucet; per iSpring, it installs from above the counter, which avoids working in the tight space under the sink.
  • Alkaline remineralization on “AK” models. The AK models include an alkaline stage that adds minerals back after the RO membrane.
  • Named certifications on several models. The RCC7AK is certified to NSF/ANSI 58 for the whole system; the RCC7AK-UV to NSF/ANSI 58 and 372; the RO500-ORB and RO500AK to NSF/ANSI 58 for TDS, per their listings. The RCC1UP-AK listing makes no NSF claim.
  • A broad lineup. Tank-based (RCC7AK), UV (RCC7AK-UV), booster-pump (RCC1UP-AK), tankless (RO500 series) and light-commercial (RCB3P).
  • Published filter schedules. The RCC7AK’s replacement sets are sold by interval: F4AK (6-month), F9K (1-year), F19K75 (2-year) and F28K75 (3-year).

What iSpring doesn’t offer in the models here: a whole-house RO system or app-connected monitoring. For a smart faucet with a TDS display, look at Waterdrop.

The iSpring Lineup Explained (Decoding the Names)

iSpring’s model names follow a pattern:

  • RCC7AK — tank-based system with an AlKaline stage (6-stage, 75 GPD).
  • RCC7AK-UV — adds a UV stage on top of alkaline (7-stage). For well water.
  • RCC1UP-AK — the UP models include a booster pump, for low water pressure. 100 GPD, with alkaline and UV stages per the listing.
  • RO500 series — the tankless line, 500 GPD. The RO500-ORB is a 3-stage version with an oil-rubbed-bronze faucet; the RO500AK adds alkaline remineralization.
  • RCB3P — 300 GPD tankless with a booster pump, for residential and light-commercial use.

Most buyers will choose among the RCC7AK, RCC7AK-UV, RCC1UP-AK, RO500 series, or RCB3P depending on water source, pressure, and whether they want a tank.

Best Overall: iSpring RCC7AK

The iSpring RCC7AK is the right iSpring pick for most homes. Per the listing, it is a 6-stage, 75 GPD tank-based system with an alkaline remineralization stage, certified to NSF/ANSI 58 for the whole system, with a top-mounted faucet that installs from above.

Best for Well Water: iSpring RCC7AK-UV

The iSpring RCC7AK-UV is the RCC7AK with a mercury-free LED UV stage added (7 stages total), and the whole system is certified to NSF/ANSI 58 and 372, per the listing. It keeps the top-mounted faucet. The listing title doesn’t state a GPD rating.

If you’re on a well, test the water first and read our whole house RO guide: well water often needs upstream treatment (sediment, iron) to protect the membrane and UV stage.

Best with Booster Pump: iSpring RCC1UP-AK

The iSpring RCC1UP-AK addresses a common RO problem: weak output. Reverse osmosis relies on water pressure to push water through the membrane, and homes with low pressure get slow production and more wastewater. Per the listing, the RCC1UP-AK adds a booster pump, a 100 GPD rating, 7 stages including alkaline and UV, and a top-mounted faucet.

If your home’s water pressure is low (check with an inexpensive pressure gauge on an outdoor spigot, or ask a plumber), a booster pump is the usual fix. The trade-offs: the pump needs an outlet under the sink, and the listing makes no NSF certification claim, unlike the RCC7AK.

Pros: Booster pump for low pressure, 100 GPD, alkaline and UV stages, top-mounted faucet. Cons: Needs an under-sink outlet, no NSF claim on the listing, more components to install.

What iSpring Could Do Better

FAQ

Is iSpring a good reverse osmosis brand?

iSpring offers one of the broadest RO lineups on Amazon, and several models document named certifications: the RCC7AK is certified to NSF/ANSI 58 for the whole system, and the RCC7AK-UV to NSF/ANSI 58 and 372, per their listings. The top-mounted faucet installs from above, per iSpring. The trade-offs are a confusing lineup and certification that varies by model. Is the iSpring RCC7AK worth it?

For most homes looking for a tank-based alkaline system, it’s a strong fit on paper: 6 stages, 75 GPD, whole-system NSF/ANSI 58 per the listing, a top-mounted faucet, and replacement sets sold by interval. Check the current price against the APEC ROES-PH75 and Express Water ROALK10D.

What’s the difference between the iSpring RCC7 and RCC7AK?

In iSpring’s naming, “AK” means an alkaline remineralization stage is included. The RCC7AK is the 6-stage version with that alkaline stage. A non-AK version omits it; check that listing for its specs. If you’re happy with plain RO water, a non-alkaline system is fine; if you want minerals added back, choose the AK.

Do I need the iSpring booster pump (RCC1UP-AK)?

Only if your home has low water pressure, which is more common on wells, upper floors, or older plumbing. RO needs pressure to push water through the membrane; without enough, output is slow and more water goes to the drain. Measure your pressure first. If it’s adequate, the RCC7AK is enough and you avoid needing an outlet under the sink.

How easy is iSpring installation?

Per iSpring, the top-mounted faucet installs from above the counter, which removes the most awkward step of a typical RO install. You still need to connect the cold-water supply and a drain line, and drill a faucet hole if your sink doesn’t have a spare opening. Stainless sinks can be drilled with the right bit; granite or quartz needs a diamond hole saw used wet at low speed, or a plumber for that step.

Does iSpring make a tankless RO system?

Yes. The RO500-ORB (500 GPD, 3-stage, 2:1, NSF/ANSI 58 per the listing) and the RO500AK (adds alkaline remineralization, auto-flush, and filter-change lights per its listing) are iSpring’s tankless line. The RCB3P is also tankless, with a booster pump. See our best tankless RO systems guide for how they compare.

Where are iSpring systems made?

The listings covered here don’t state where the systems are made or assembled. If country of origin matters to you, check the listing’s product details or ask iSpring before buying.
