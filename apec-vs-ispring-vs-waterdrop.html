Head-to-Head: The Dimensions That Decide It

Up-Front Cost

Prices on all three move around, so compare current prices on the listings before you decide. As a general pattern, tankless systems with an electric pump and a smart faucet sit at a higher price point than basic tank systems like the APEC and iSpring. Whether the tankless extras are worth it to you is the central question here.

Tip: Check current prices; don’t rely on old price snapshots.

Filtration & Certifications

All three use a reverse osmosis membrane, which is what does the heavy lifting on dissolved solids. The differences are in stage count and which certifications each listing claims:

  • APEC ROES-50: 5 stages. The listing makes no NSF certification claim for this model.
  • iSpring RCC7AK: 6 stages including alkaline remineralization. Certified to NSF/ANSI 58 for the whole system, per the listing.
  • Waterdrop G3P600: 8 stages. Certified to NSF/ANSI 58 (TDS) and 372 (lead-free materials), and “tested and certified against NSF 42” (chlorine, taste, odor), per the listing. The listing does not claim NSF/ANSI 53.

On paper, the Waterdrop lists the most standards. If you have a specific contaminant concern, test your water first and check that the system you pick is certified for that contaminant.

Edge: Waterdrop (most certifications listed), then iSpring (NSF/ANSI 58).

Water Taste (The Alkaline Factor)

A common complaint about RO water is that it tastes “flat,” because the membrane removes most dissolved minerals. Here’s where iSpring differs from the other two:

  • iSpring RCC7AK: Alkaline remineralization stage built in, which adds some minerals back after the membrane.
  • APEC ROES-50: No remineralization in the base model. (APEC sells a separate alkaline model, the ROES-PH75, if you want it from them.)
  • Waterdrop G3P600: No alkaline stage listed for the G3P600; Waterdrop sells alkaline models such as the G5P500A and X16 (see below).

If remineralized water out of the box matters to you, the iSpring is the only one of these three that includes it.

Edge: iSpring (alkaline stage built in).

Flow & Capacity (Tankless vs Tank)

This is where the tankless design matters:

  • Waterdrop G3P600: Tankless, 600 GPD. There’s no storage tank: water is filtered on demand, so there’s no tank to run down and wait on. It also frees up the cabinet space a tank would take (the unit itself is 18.12″ L x 5.67″ W x 17.72″ H per the listing).
  • iSpring RCC7AK: Tank-based, 75 GPD membrane feeding a storage tank. Faster membrane than the APEC.
  • APEC ROES-50: Tank-based, 50 GPD membrane feeding a storage tank. The slowest membrane of the three.

For many households a storage tank covers normal drinking and cooking use. For a large family that draws a lot of water at once, the tankless Waterdrop (or at least the iSpring’s faster 75 GPD membrane) is the more comfortable choice.

Edge: Waterdrop (tankless, 600 GPD), iSpring second.

Wastewater Ratio

RO systems send some water to the drain. A higher pure-to-drain ratio means less wastewater for the same amount of drinking water:

  • Waterdrop G3P600: 2:1 pure-to-drain, per the listing.
  • iSpring RCC7AK & APEC ROES-50: Neither listing states a pure-to-drain ratio. Conventional tank systems without a pump generally send more water to the drain than they produce.

If wastewater matters to you (septic system, water costs, drought rules), a stated ratio like the Waterdrop’s is easier to plan around.

Edge: Waterdrop (the only one of the three with a stated ratio).

Installation

All three are designed for DIY under-sink installation, but the requirements differ:

  • iSpring RCC7AK: Per iSpring, the top-mounted faucet installs from above the counter, which avoids working on the faucet from inside the cabinet.
  • Waterdrop G3P600: No tank to position, but it needs an electrical outlet under the sink.
  • APEC ROES-50: Standard tank-based install with quick-connect fittings, per the listing. No outlet needed.

Edge: iSpring (faucet installs from above, per iSpring). Waterdrop has no tank but needs power.

Filter Replacement

Filters are the ongoing cost. Before you buy, look up the current price of each system’s replacement set and how often it’s due:

  • APEC ROES-50: Filter set FILTER-MAX-ES50; APEC says to replace filters every 6–24 months (the stages have different intervals).
  • iSpring RCC7AK: iSpring sells replacement sets sized by time: F4AK (6-month), F9K (1-year), F19K75 (2-year) and F28K75 (3-year).
  • Waterdrop G3P600: Uses Waterdrop’s own cartridges; the faucet shows filter life, per the listing. Check the replacement filter listings for current prices and intervals.

Tip: Add up the cost of filters over three to five years, not just the system price.

Smart Features

  • Waterdrop G3P600: Smart faucet that shows TDS and filter life, per the listing. The listing does not mention leak detection.
  • iSpring RCC7AK & APEC ROES-50: No smart features. No electronics to fail and no outlet needed.

If you want a TDS readout and a filter-life reminder at the faucet, Waterdrop is the only one of the three that offers it. If you want leak protection on any system, a separate mechanical leak-stop valve is an option.

Edge: Waterdrop (only one with smart features).

Model Variants Worth Knowing

Each of these three is really a family, not a single product. If the headline model isn’t quite right, here’s the variant to look at instead:

APEC — The ROES-50 is the base. If you want alkaline remineralization, APEC makes the ROES-PH75: 6 stages including a calcium alkaline stage, 75 GPD, and a WQA-certified system, per the listing. There’s also the RO-90: 5 stages, 90 GPD, WQA Gold Seal, and “US assembled” per the listing. And the RO-PERM adds a permeate pump for low-pressure homes.

iSpring — The RCC7AK is the alkaline model. The RCC7AK-UV adds a mercury-free LED UV stage (7 stages total, whole system certified to NSF/ANSI 58 and 372 per the listing) — the version to look at if you’re on well water or any unchlorinated source.

Waterdrop — The G3P600 is the mainstream pick. Other models include the G5P500A (500 GPD tankless with alkaline remineralization, 2:1), the G3P800 (800 GPD, 10 stages, 3:1, certified by IAPMO R&T to NSF/ANSI 58, 42, 53 and 372 per the listing) and the X16 (1,600 GPD peak, 11 stages with alkaline, 3:1). For countertop, no-plumbing situations, there’s the M6H Instant Hot. Our full Waterdrop review covers the family.

The takeaway: if one brand’s philosophy appeals but the specific model is off — too slow, no alkaline, no UV — there’s almost always a sibling that fixes it without switching brands.

Which Should You Buy?

Buy the APEC ROES-50 if: you want a simple, conventional tank-based system with no electronics and no outlet needed, and you don’t specifically need remineralization, certifications or tankless flow. Note that its listing makes no NSF claim; if certification matters to you, look at the iSpring or Waterdrop.

Buy the iSpring RCC7AK if: you want remineralized water (alkaline stage), a faster 75 GPD membrane, NSF/ANSI 58 certification per the listing, and a faucet that installs from above — in a tank-based system.

Buy the Waterdrop G3P600 if: you want tankless design (no tank, more cabinet space), 600 GPD on demand, a stated 2:1 drain ratio, and a faucet that shows TDS and filter life — and you have an outlet under the sink. FAQ

Is APEC or iSpring better?

Both are tank-based under-sink RO systems. APEC’s ROES-50 is the plainer 5-stage, 50 GPD design, and its listing makes no NSF claim. iSpring’s RCC7AK adds alkaline remineralization, a faster 75 GPD membrane, NSF/ANSI 58 certification per the listing, and a faucet that installs from above per iSpring. If you want the simplest system, get the APEC ROES-50; if you want remineralization and a certified system, get the iSpring RCC7AK.

Is the Waterdrop G3P600 worth the extra cost over APEC or iSpring?

It depends on what you value. The Waterdrop’s advantages are on its listing: tankless design (more cabinet space, no tank to run down), 600 GPD, a 2:1 pure-to-drain ratio, NSF/ANSI 58 and 372 certification, and a faucet that shows TDS and filter life. If those matter to you, especially the tankless design and water efficiency, the premium can make sense. If you just want RO drinking water from a simple system, the APEC or iSpring covers that for less.

Which one remineralizes the water?

The iSpring RCC7AK, because it’s the only one of the three with an alkaline remineralization stage built in. RO water can taste “flat” because the membrane removes minerals; the alkaline stage adds some back. The APEC ROES-50 and Waterdrop G3P600 don’t include remineralization, though both brands sell alkaline models.

Which is simplest to install?

Per iSpring, the iSpring RCC7AK’s top-mounted faucet installs from above, which avoids the most awkward part of an RO install. The Waterdrop G3P600 has no tank to position but needs an electrical outlet under the sink. The APEC ROES-50 is a standard tank-based install with quick-connect fittings, per the listing.

Do any of these work for well water?

All three can filter well water, but well water usually has no disinfection, and an RO membrane alone isn’t a guaranteed barrier against bacteria and viruses. For well water, look for a UV stage. iSpring makes the RCC7AK-UV, which adds a mercury-free LED UV stage, and the Express Water ROALKUV10D (11 stages, alkaline plus UV, leak detector included, per the listing) is another UV-equipped option. Well water also often needs pre-treatment (sediment, iron) before any RO system. Test first — see our well water RO guide and whole house RO guide.

Can any of these filter the whole house?

No — all three are point-of-use under-sink systems that treat one tap (usually the kitchen). For RO water at every tap, you need a whole-house RO system, which is a different and much larger category. See our best whole house RO systems guide and our whole house vs under-sink RO decision guide.

Bottom Line

There’s no single winner here, only the best fit for your priorities:

  • Optimize for simplicity → APEC ROES-50
  • Optimize for remineralization and a certified tank system → iSpring RCC7AK
  • Optimize for tankless flow and water efficiency → Waterdrop G3P600

Pick the approach that fits your priorities, sink cabinet and budget.
