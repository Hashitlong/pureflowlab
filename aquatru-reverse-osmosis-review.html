About AquaTru

AquaTru sells countertop water purification systems. The pitch is RO drinking water without plumbing or an under-sink install.

What the listings document:

  • Countertop only. Every model covered here sits on the counter and is filled by hand. None connects to your plumbing.
  • Third-party certification. The Classic is tested and certified by IAPMO to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 and P473, per its listing. The Glass Carafe listings say “certified to NSF standards by IAPMO” without naming the standards.
  • 4-stage filtration on the Carafe. The Glass Carafe listing describes 4 stages: a pre/carbon filter, the RO membrane, and a VOC filter.
  • “84 contaminants.” Per the Classic listing, the 84 contaminants covered by its certifications include PFOA/PFOS and microplastics. The Glass Carafe title also says 84 contaminants.

What AquaTru doesn’t offer in this lineup: under-sink systems, whole-house RO, or hot water. For those, look at Waterdrop, iSpring, APEC, or Express Water.

The AquaTru Lineup Explained

AquaTru’s current countertop lineup is simple:

  • AquaTru Classic — the original countertop unit. Certified by IAPMO to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 and P473 per the listing; filters last 600–1,200 gallons (6 months to 2 years).
  • AquaTru Glass Carafe — the newer model that dispenses into a glass carafe. 4-stage, with published filter intervals for each stage.
  • AquaTru Glass Carafe Smart — the Glass Carafe plus a WiFi app (North America only) that shows filter life and TDS.

AquaTru does NOT make under-sink RO, whole-house RO, or a hot-water dispensing model (the Waterdrop M6H offers hot water).

The choice comes down to three things: whether you want a glass carafe, whether you want the WiFi app, and which certification statement matters to you (the Classic’s listing names the standards; the Carafe listings don’t).

What Sets AquaTru Apart

1. A named certification stack on the Classic. The Classic is tested and certified by IAPMO to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 and P473, per the listing. Not every countertop unit can say that: the SimPure Y7P-BW listing, for example, says it was tested by SGS against NSF/ANSI 58 rather than certified.

2. No plumbing at all. Nothing to drill, no drain connection. It works in rentals and can move with you.

3. Published filter intervals. The listings give filter life in both months and gallons, which makes the running cost easy to estimate.

What AquaTru Could Do Better

1. No under-sink option. AquaTru doesn’t make a plumbed-in product.

2. Countertop convenience has a cost. Compare current prices: an under-sink RO system can produce far more water on demand for a similar outlay, if you’re able to install one.

3. Filters are a recurring cost. Check the price of replacement filters before buying, and multiply by how often you’ll need them.

4. Batch production. A countertop unit filters a tankful at a time. If several people fill water bottles at once, you may have to wait for the next batch.

5. No hot-water option. The Waterdrop M6H adds instant hot water; AquaTru doesn’t offer that.

AquaTru vs Competitors

vs Waterdrop M6H Instant Hot: The Waterdrop M6H is a 7-stage countertop RO unit with instant hot water (room temperature up to 203°F, 5 presets), a 3:1 pure-to-drain ratio, and a 40 oz glass pitcher, per its listing. Its listing makes no certification claim (it says “tested by a third-party laboratory”). Choose the M6H if you want hot water; choose the AquaTru Classic if you want named NSF/ANSI certifications.

vs Waterdrop G3P600 (under-sink): The most important comparison. The Waterdrop G3P600 is an under-sink tankless system rated at 600 GPD with a 2:1 pure-to-drain ratio, certified to NSF/ANSI 58 and 372 per its listing. It needs an outlet under the sink and a faucet install. If you can plumb under-sink, it gives you RO water on demand; AquaTru makes the most sense when under-sink isn’t an option.

vs carbon pitcher filters: Carbon pitcher and gravity filters are a different technology. They are generally designed for taste, odor, chlorine and specific certified contaminants, and they don’t reduce total dissolved solids the way an RO membrane does. If you want RO water specifically, a countertop RO unit is the no-plumbing way to get it; check any filter’s own NSF/ANSI certifications for the contaminants you care about.

FAQ

Is AquaTru worth the price?

For renters, apartment dwellers, or anyone who can’t install under-sink plumbing, it’s one of the few countertop RO options with named NSF/ANSI certifications (on the Classic, per its listing). If you can plumb under-sink, compare an under-sink system such as the Waterdrop G3P600 first: it produces water on demand rather than in batches. Check current prices on both, including replacement filters.

What’s the difference between the AquaTru Classic and the Glass Carafe?

The Classic is the original unit, and its listing names IAPMO certification to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 and P473. The Glass Carafe dispenses into glass, has 4 stages with published per-stage filter intervals, and its listing says “certified to NSF standards by IAPMO” without naming them. The Glass Carafe Smart adds a WiFi app.

Does AquaTru really remove 84 contaminants?

Per the Classic’s listing, AquaTru is tested and certified by IAPMO to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 and P473, covering 84 contaminants including PFOA/PFOS and microplastics. You can check any certification claim in the certifier’s public listings. As with any system, certification applies to the specific contaminants and reduction levels listed, not to every contaminant.

How often do you have to replace AquaTru filters?

For the Glass Carafe, per the listing: pre/carbon filter every 6 months or 300 gallons, RO filter every year or 600 gallons, VOC filter every year or 300 gallons. For the Classic, the listing says filters last 600–1,200 gallons (6 months to 2 years). Check current filter prices to estimate your yearly cost.

Can AquaTru filter well water?

Possibly, with caveats. RO membranes foul quickly on untreated water with iron, manganese, or hydrogen sulfide, and any RO system should only be used on water that is microbiologically safe or disinfected. If you’re on a well, test your water first and treat those problems upstream. Expect shorter filter life on hard or high-sediment water.

Is AquaTru worth it vs a Brita pitcher?

They are different products. A carbon pitcher is mainly for taste, odor and chlorine, plus whatever specific contaminants its own certifications list; it doesn’t reduce TDS the way an RO membrane does. If you want RO water, compare the certified contaminant lists for both products.

How big is AquaTru on the counter?

Check the dimensions on the listing for the model you’re considering. Leave room to remove the tank or carafe for filling.

Does AquaTru have an under-sink RO?

No. AquaTru’s lineup is countertop. For under-sink RO, look at Waterdrop, APEC (ROES-50X), iSpring, or Express Water. See our best reverse osmosis systems guide for the under-sink comparison.

Bottom Line: Which AquaTru Should You Buy?

For most countertop RO buyers, the AquaTru Classic is the pick, mainly because its listing names IAPMO certification to NSF/ANSI 42, 53, 58, 401 and P473.

If you want a glass carafe, the AquaTru Glass Carafe is the better fit, with published filter intervals for each stage.

If you’d use app tracking, the AquaTru Glass Carafe Smart adds filter-life and TDS readouts via WiFi (North America only).

Before you buy: if you can install under-sink RO in your kitchen, compare the Waterdrop G3P600, a 600 GPD tankless system certified to NSF/ANSI 58 and 372 per its listing. AquaTru is the right call when “no plumbing” is a hard requirement.
